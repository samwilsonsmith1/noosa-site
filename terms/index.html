noosa is a free, opinionated local guide to Noosa, Queensland. It suggests places, walks, beaches, food, drinks, events and ways to spend a day, and it changes those suggestions with the weather and the time of day.

Everything in it is general information and personal opinion, not professional advice. It isn't safety, medical, legal, financial, travel or navigation advice, and it isn't an official source of information from any government, council, park, lifesaving or tourism body. You decide what to do. Always use your own judgement, and check conditions, signs and official advice before you go anywhere or do anything.

2. Information can be wrong or out of date

We work hard to keep noosa accurate, but we can't promise that anything in it is complete, current or correct. In particular:

  • Weather, sea and sun. Temperature, rain, wind, UV, sea temperature, swell and surf come from forecast services (weather data by Open-Meteo.com) and our own estimates. Forecasts are often wrong, and conditions change quickly. The app may suggest a beach when it starts raining, or a walk on a day that turns hot or stormy. Check the Bureau of Meteorology (bom.gov.au) for current forecasts and warnings.
  • Tides are estimated from forecast sea levels and are approximate. Never use noosa's tides for boating, fishing, navigation or crossing the Noosa bar. Use official tide tables and Maritime Safety Queensland advice.
  • Surf, water clarity, bluebottles and lifeguard hours are estimates or general guides, not observations. Lifeguard patrols change with the season and the day; check the flags and Beachsafe (beachsafe.org.au).

3. Your safety is your responsibility

Noosa's beaches, rivers, headlands, bush and hinterland are beautiful and can be dangerous. Whatever the app suggests, you choose whether to go and how you do it, and you're responsible for your own safety and the safety of anyone in your care, especially children.

  • Swimming. Only swim at patrolled beaches, between the red and yellow flags, while lifeguards are on duty, and follow their directions. Where the app says there are no lifeguards, swimming isn't advised. Rips, waves, currents, sharks, bluebottles, stingrays, stingers, submerged rocks and sandbanks can injure or kill. The Noosa River mouth (the Spit) has strong currents and bull sharks: wade, don't swim. Never swim alone, at night, after drinking, or in murky water after rain.
  • Rocks, cliffs and caves. Rock platforms, headlands, cliffs and sea caves (for example around Hells Gates and Paradise Caves) can be deadly. Waves can wash people off rocks without warning, rocks are slippery and loose, and tides can cut off access. Where the app says somewhere isn't advised, it means don't go down there.
  • Walks and the outdoors. Stay on marked tracks, walk in daylight, carry water, wear sun protection and proper shoes, and tell someone where you're going. Heat, falls, snakes, insects, wildlife, fallen trees and getting lost are real risks. Check track closures and fire danger before you go.
  • Water activities and tours. Boating, jet skis, kayaking, paddleboarding, surfing, fishing, 4WD beach driving, skydiving, ballooning and other activities carry risk. Use licensed operators, follow their safety briefings, wear life jackets, and hold any licence, permit or registration the law requires (for example a boat licence above certain engine sizes, or a vehicle access permit for beach driving). Check tides before driving on the beach.
  • Alcohol and driving. Bars, breweries, happy hours and drinks tours are listed for adults of legal drinking age. Drink responsibly, never drink and drive, swim or boat, and arrange a safe way home.
  • Health. Think about your own health, fitness, allergies and medical conditions before any activity or food. The app doesn't know them.
  • Emergencies. In an emergency call 000 (or 112 from a mobile). Don't rely on the app in an emergency.
